# Stratton et al. (2026) — Human-motion prediction quality and navigation

## Bibliographic verification

  HRI 2026 paper, pages 1060--1069, published 16 March 2026, DOI

- [Author-available version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09856) reports the same

## Method and evidence

The study evaluates how human-motion-prediction quality affects robot navigation
and human impressions in constrained spaces. It uses a two-person scenario, two
robot platforms, two sites and a within-subject study with 80 participants. The
reported conclusion is that average displacement error alone does not reliably
predict navigation or human-experience outcomes, and that cooperation cannot be
assumed in constrained interaction.

## Consequence for the gap and evaluation

This source weakens any claim that prediction error metrics alone establish
closed-loop navigation value. The CCA study should therefore retain context
calibration/OOD analysis and report control outcomes separately from LSTM error.
It is an evaluation-boundary source, not a direct fixed-budget allocator
comparator.
